01

Install

Get Hyprtile on Google Play. Pay once: no subscription, no ads, no analytics, no account. Hyprtile runs on Android 10 or newer, on phones and tablets, in portrait and landscape.

Updates. A new version from Google Play downloads in the background while you use your phone. When it is ready, a banner says "an update is ready", with restart and later. Later hides it until you next come home. Home starts faster too, from a startup profile that ships with the app.

02

First run and setup

The first start has three screens, one choice each. The title tells you where you are, like 1 of 3 · pick your look.

  1. pick your look: every look as a card, in four groups. Tap one and your home changes behind the screen. Then next ›.
  2. pick your pages: the free ready workspaces as cards. Tick up to three; at a glance and music are ticked to begin with. They take the place of the starter pages. Untick them all and you get keep the starter pages ›.
  3. make it your home: tap make it my home and pick Hyprtile when Android asks which app opens on home. Then take the tour ›, or later, take the tour ›.

Each screen has ‹ back. The first run asks for no permission. Location, calendar and notification access are asked the first time a window needs them. People who update from an older Hyprtile never see the first run and keep their pages.

The setup guide

While the first steps are not done, the main menu starts with finish setup, with how many are left. The setup guide has 8 steps; the first three count for finish setup. Tap a step to open its page. Done steps show a tick.

  1. default home: make Hyprtile your home screen.
  2. lock screen: pick Hyprtile lock screen, then "Lock screen" or "Home and lock screen".
  3. notification access: for the notifications window and music controls.
  4. hide system cards: set lock screen notifications to "Don't show any notifications".
  5. dynamic clock off: so Android's big clock does not cover yours.
  6. location for weather: or type a city instead.
  7. calendar: for the calendar and meeting windows.
  8. battery saver: advice on saving power.

The guide is in the menu again under tour and gestures, setup guide, or type setup in the terminal.

03

Set as home screen

The easiest way is the third screen of the first run, make it my home, or later the setup step default home. You can also do it in Android settings. On most phones it is under Apps, then Default apps, then Home app. To go back, pick your old launcher there.

04

The tour and the cheat sheet

The tour starts by itself at the end of the first run. It has 8 steps on your real desktop, and edit mode comes first, since the layout is locked outside it:

  1. hold an empty spot: edit mode. Keep your finger still until it buzzes.
  2. tap + add: pick a window. It lands in free space at its own size. Hold one and drag it out to drop it where you like.
  3. drag a window: drop it in the middle of another to swap, on its edge to split, or on free space to move it.
  4. drag a gap or an edge: resize. The windows beside it follow.
  5. tap done: done saves the layout, and outside edit mode nothing moves by accident.
  6. swipe sideways: move between pages.
  7. double tap a window: it fills the screen. Double tap again to tile it back.
  8. swipe up from the bottom: find an app. Type to filter, tap to launch.

A ghost finger acts each one out on your own windows, and a step is done when you do it yourself. The card has back, skip and end. TalkBack hears each step.

The end card, tour done, offers the gesture cheat sheet, and undo the tour if the tour changed your layout.

The gestures cheat sheet lists every gesture with what it does and how. Find both in the menu under tour and gestures, or type tour or gestures in the terminal.

06

The desktop

Your home is a tiling desktop, like Hyprland on a laptop. Each window is live, with a gradient border, a soft glow and frosted glass that lets the wallpaper shine through.

The bar. A waybar style bar with workspace pills, the clock, battery, network, cpu and memory. On a fresh install it is hidden, like in the store pictures, so windows get the space. Show it in the menu under home and apps, top bar. With the bar shown, the ≡ button on it opens the menu. The phone's own status bar has its own row there too.

Top bar items. Menu, home and apps, top bar items: pick what the bar shows, and put each item left, in the centre or right, in the order you like, like waybar. Pick the clock format: 12 or 24 hour, date, seconds, or your own pattern in hyprtile.conf, bar, clock_format. Auto shows the date when it fits.

The dock lines up with the windows and takes all its apps, in two rows when needed. It shows every app you pin, up to 10, even with more pins than how many says, and its icons stay round with six apps or more. Work apps get their own icons and pins, and an app you uninstall leaves the dock. With three button navigation, the buttons sit on a solid band.

The starter desktop has four workspaces, if you keep the starter pages in the first run:

  1. clock, weather, battery and moon, placed on a grid with room to add more
  2. btop, cliamp, fetch and toggles
  3. calendar, notes, pomodoro and moon
  4. an empty page

Pick pages in the first run instead, and you get those pages. At a glance and music are ticked to begin with.

09

Add a window

  1. Tap + add, or a dotted + spot.
  2. The sheet shows windows in groups: time and weather, phone, media, system, tools and pro. Each has a live preview, one line about it and the size it opens at, like 4x2. Tap search windows to type. The list glides on after a flick.
  3. Tap a window. Aimed at a spot, it goes straight in. Otherwise it waits at the best spot with place for me, spots › and cancel, and you can drag it anywhere.
  4. Or hold a row and drag: the window comes out under your finger, with a lit outline of where it lands.

A new window opens at its own size and never takes more than half the page.

Replace with

Hold a window, let go, and tap replace with. The same sheet opens, with every other window's live preview. The window you replace is pictured at the top with a now tag, and each row says swap. Tap one and it takes the same place and size. Pro kinds open the pro menu without pro.

Android widgets

With pro, pick android widget in the sheet. The chooser is a gallery: each app is a heading, and each of its widgets is a card with the picture the app ships for it (or the app's icon when it ships none), its name and the size it opens at. From replace with, a widget takes the window's place and size, and one widget can replace another. Some widgets ask you to set them up first.

10

Move and size on a free page

A free page is a grid of cells, like an Android home screen: about 4x8 on a phone and 8x12 on a tablet. Empty cells stay empty.

  • Move: hold a window and drag it to empty space.
  • Swap: drop it on the middle of another window.
  • Split: drop it near another window's edge to share that space.
  • Size: in edit mode drag an edge handle, or use the window menu, size ›: wider, narrower, taller, shorter.
  • By taps only: the window menu has move with a spots list and size ›, so nothing needs a drag.

While you drag, the drop is named: "swap", "split 2x2", or the size.

Landscape. Turn the phone and a free page fits the screen. Edit it in landscape and it keeps its own landscape layout, while portrait stays as it was. rotation in home and apps steps through follow phone, portrait, landscape and always turns.

11

Tiled pages

A tiled page fills the whole screen with its windows, like Hyprland. Pick the layout in the menu, home and apps, how windows share the page: each new one halves (dwindle) or one big, rest stacked (master).

  • Drag a gap between two windows in edit mode to resize. Every window on both sides follows, and the split snaps to the middle with a tick.
  • Double tap a gap in edit mode to flip that split: side by side or stacked.
  • Double tap a window for full screen, and again to tile it back.
  • reset splits in the window menu puts sizes and flips back.

Each workspace keeps its own sizes.

The app shelf

The app shelf is in the phone group of + add. It holds one app per cell, at any size: 1x1 is one app, a 4x1 is a row of four, a 1x4 is a column, and 4x2 or bigger is a grid. It opens as a 4x1 row. Each shelf keeps its own apps, so you can have as many as you like.

  • Add an app: tap a + on the shelf and pick from add to the shelf. Type to find one.
  • Open an app: tap its icon.
  • Change the shelf: hold an app and let go. The window menu starts with open, change app ›, move left, move right and remove from shelf, then apps on the shelf › and labels.
  • Size: size › in the same menu, or the edge handles in edit mode. A smaller shelf keeps the apps it cannot show for when it grows.
  • Labels: auto, on or off. Auto shows names when there is room. The same row is in the menu, home and apps, app shelf labels.

The icons follow the dock's icons style: color, theme or mono. TalkBack reads "app shelf, 4 apps, 4 by 1" and each app by name, and apps on the shelf lets you reorder by taps.

School holidays

The holidays window shows school breaks next to the public holidays. The dates come from OpenHolidays, for about 36 countries, per country and per zone: a German state, a French zone, a Swiss canton, a Belgian community or a Dutch region. They are fetched once a month and kept on the phone. While school is out, the big number gives way to "school's out", with a ring that fills as the break goes and the day school starts again.

Hold the window and let go. After country and region come the school rows:

school holidays
with holidays (the default), only school or off. Step it with ‹ and ›.
school zone
where your school is. pick for me guesses from your region; a long list opens with the keyboard, so type to find yours. A country with one set of dates says whole country, and a country the feed lacks says not in the feed.
add a break of your own
for a private school, or a country the feed lacks. Type day month day month and a name, like 23 12 6 1 christmas, or two dates, like 2027-02-15 2027-02-19. One date is a single day. A break is at most 120 days long; if Hyprtile can not read it, it says why and keeps your text.
your breaks
the breaks you typed, by date. Tap one to remove it.

asks the feeds again

In what it shows, school breaks in the list, school band on the month and school bars on the strip turn each part on or off. Each break gets its own art: a summer sun, autumn leaves, a tree, a ski slope or a spring flower. The same breaks show in a holidays window on the lock screen and in the holidays stat wallpaper, from one shared fetch.

Rofi: find an app

Swipe up from the lower half of the screen. Start typing, and fuzzy search finds the app. Apps are listed most opened first; switch to a to z in the menu, home and apps, app order.

A swipe that starts on a window that scrolls, like apps, scrolls that window instead. Every list glides on after a flick, and a touch stops it.

Turn on open the only match in home and apps, and the app opens by itself once your typing leaves just one.

With pro, contacts in search: type a name in rofi to call or text. Off until you turn it on.

16b

Termux scripts

Early access: Insiders have it now, and from 1 February 2027 it is part of pro. Hyprtile does not bring Linux with it. It asks your own Termux to run a command and shows what comes back. Use the Termux from F-Droid or GitHub: the Google Play one cannot take commands from other apps.

  1. Open the menu, customize, for ricers, termux.
  2. get termux if you have none.
  3. turn on commands copies one line and opens Termux. Paste it there and press enter.
  4. allow hyprtile, then test. It says ok when all is ready.

Then type t and a command in kitty, like t ls. Or add a script window: pick a script from ~/.shortcuts (the folder Termux:Widget uses) or type a command. It shows what the script prints, colours and all, and runs it again on a timer while it is on screen. Hold it for its menu: run every, name, text size, start at the top or the end, time out, and which parts it shows. Drag to scroll.

Seven ready widgets (bar, btop, weather, ping, colours, calendar and crypto) install with one line from termux.zanoski.com/widgets. The whole setup is at termux.zanoski.com. Android hides cpu load and uptime from apps, so scripts that read them show nothing; memory, disk, network and anything from the web work.

Only apps you allow can send commands to Termux, and only after you turn commands on. Hyprtile runs only what you type in kitty or set in a script window, and nothing leaves your phone.

Music, radio and cava

cliamp

The music window, styled after cliamp by Bjarne Øverli (MIT): track and artist, time, Playing or Paused, a block spectrum, a seek bar, three keys and the playlist when the player shares one. Keys follow what the player allows. Tap a playlist row to jump to it.

More than one player? Swipe the track line or the [app] tag sideways to switch players. Tap the tag to open that app, and hold it for the list of players. Hyprtile stays on the app you last started, even between songs.

Hold the window and let go for real visualizer, spectrum, music source, queue and radio ›. album art puts the cover behind the player, and album art blur sets it sharp, light or soft. cava has the same two rows.

Radio

cliamp radio is built in: Lofi, Synthwave, EDM, Chiptunes, Amiga, NCS House, Dubstep, Drum and Bass, Trap, Phonk, Chill and more. Pick a station, see the song title as it plays, and step stations with next and previous. Radio by cliamp, radio.cliamp.stream. It connects only while a station plays.

cava

A visualizer in seven styles: bars, blocks, mirror, wave, radial, term and dots. cava shows only the sound; turn on the track and play keys in what it shows. Colors run vertical, across or mono. The window menu has cava look ›, music source and go to player. Swipe cava sideways to switch players, or hold its title for the list.

The real visualizer

Turn on real visualizer in the menu, home and apps. Android asks for the microphone, because that permission reads the sound the phone plays. Only the loudness per band is read; nothing is kept or sent. Some players keep their sound private, and then the window says "no audio from this player".

Weather and places

Weather comes from Open Meteo. With location allowed it uses where you are, rounded to about 1 km before it is sent. Or set a city in the menu, clock and weather, weather places, or from the weather window menu. Then no location permission is needed.

forecast model, in clock and weather, picks the weather model: auto, MET Norway, ECMWF, DWD ICON, GFS or Météo France. Auto takes the best one for where you are. The conf key is weather_model under general.

calendar app, in clock and weather, picks which app opens your events and days, so Android stops asking each time.

Several places work too, split by commas: Zagreb, London, here, where here is your location. Tap the weather window to step to the next place. A tall weather window shows a pill per place and + city; hold a pill to show, move or remove it. Tap a day in the days row to see that day.

hyprtile.conf

Hyprtile keeps its look in hyprtile.conf, written in Hyprland's syntax. Open it from the menu, config file, or type conf in the terminal. Save it and the desktop reloads live.

theme = navy
wallpaper = navy
wallpaper_weather = off   # on: the real weather over the wallpaper
theme_auto = off          # off, sun or hours (22 to 7)
theme_day = catppuccin-latte
theme_night =

general {
    gaps_in = 5
    gaps_out = 12
    border_size = 3
    col.active_border = theme 45deg
    layout = dwindle      # dwindle or master
    lock_gesture = off    # off or double_tap
}

decoration {
    rounding = 16
    blur {
        enabled = true
        size = 8
    }
}

workspace = 1, grid 4x8, clock 4x2+0+0, weather 4x3+0+2
workspace = 2, btop, cliamp, fetch, toggles
  • theme, wallpaper, wallpaper_weather, theme_auto, theme_day, theme_night: the look on top of the file
  • general: gaps, border size and gradient border colours
  • decoration: rounding, opacity, blur, glow and shadow
  • fonts: the terminal font, the clock face and its weight
  • animations: bezier curves and the animations that use them
  • bar: show or hide, top or bottom, modules and opacity
  • workspace: what each page opens. grid 4x8 makes a free page; each box is width x height + column + row.
  • windows: world clock cities, pomodoro minutes, cliamp and cava options, shelf_labels
  • lockscreen: the clock, glow, seconds, style and what the lock screen shows

The file itself explains every key in comments.

Motion and battery

Menu, fine tune, motion and battery:

frame rate
60 fps (the default), max (your screen's top rate) or 30 fps. It sets everything that moves on its own. Touches, drags and scrolling stay fast.
animations
off stills the scene, the border spin, ripples and transitions
touch ripples
a neon ring where your finger lands
frame stats
shows the frame rate on screen

Windows with nothing new to show do not redraw, and an open menu, rofi or the picker rests between changes. The bar, the dock and the weather redraw only when something changed.

Battery saver brings motion to about 30 frames a second and holds the weather sky still, unless you picked a frame rate yourself. A page where nothing moves, like a black or image wallpaper with still windows, drops to one frame a second.

Live weather costs about half a point of CPU while rain, snow, fog or thunder falls, and nothing under a clear sky.

To save the most: frame rate 30, animations off, a black page, or the oled void or eink paper look. With animations off the weather sky stays still too. Solid one colour borders never redraw on their own.

Link devices

Menu, home and apps, link devices syncs your theme with Phosphor or another Hyprtile on the same wifi, and with an Omarchy computer through the Phone Sync plugin. Turn link on, pick the device, and check that both screens show the same six digit code, then same code, pair.

On Android 17 it asks for local network access, after a short note, when you turn link on. It stays on your own network and only sends the theme and this phone's name to devices you paired. It only answers devices on the same wifi or ethernet, never over mobile data. It is off by default. Full steps on the Phone Sync page.

Accessibility

  • Touch targets are at least 48dp, even on a window drawn zoomed out.
  • TalkBack reads menu rows, picker rows, the edit strip, handles and every window. A double tap on a window opens its menu.
  • Nothing in placement needs a drag: the size and spots menus do it all by taps.
  • The glass under text keeps a 7:1 contrast. Themes keep their original colours.
  • The clock is lifted to 3:1 against the wallpaper under it, the rule for large text.
  • The system font size is honoured: windows zoom with it.
  • Android's "remove animations" and the animations setting still the motion.
  • Lock gesture lets you lock the phone with a double tap, and lock phone in the pages menu does it with one tap.
  • Menus follow large font sizes, and the support row fits on one line with large text.

Privacy and permissions

No ads, no tracking, no analytics, no crash reports, no error reports, no account and no AI. Your data stays on your phone. Menu, privacy and permissions opens the privacy page. It says each thing once:

  • promises: the list above, as pills
  • permissions: each one Android asks you for, what it is for, and whether it is allowed now. Tap a row to open Android's page for Hyprtile, where you can take it back.
  • given without asking: the ones Android gives on install, each with what it is for
  • denied by design: SMS, call log, phone and your files. Hyprtile never asks for them; a wallpaper or font you pick comes through Android's picker.
  • only when you turn it on: what goes out, and only once you turn it on
  • in full: the privacy policy, with every host and what it receives

Hyprtile holds 23 permissions: 13 that Android asks you for, and 10 that it gives without asking. Every asked one is optional:

location
weather for where you are, rounded to about 1 km before it is sent. A typed weather place works without it. Precise only for the gps window (pro), and that never leaves the phone.
calendar
the calendar windows read your events on the phone, nothing is written
notification access
the notifications window, badges and the music controls. Read on the phone, never sent.
microphone
only for real audio bars in cava and cliamp: it reads how loud the music is. Nothing is recorded, kept or sent.
contacts
only for contacts in search (pro). Never saved or sent.
camera
only to scan a 2fa QR code (pro). No picture is kept.
nearby devices
the devices window (pro): names and battery of paired gear. It never scans.
health and activity
the health window (pro) and steps on the lock screen, read only
notifications
the radio's controls in the shade
exact alarms
the pomodoro rings on time from the background
local network
link and the router ping
accessibility service
only for the lock gesture: it locks the screen and reads nothing
run commands in termux
only for Termux scripts: kitty's t and script windows ask your Termux to run what you set. Output stays on the phone.

Given without asking: internet, alarms, do not disturb, uninstall apps, pull down the shade, vibrate, network state, audio settings, radio playback and Google Play billing.

Location, calendar and notification access are asked only the first time a window needs them. Microphone, contacts, clipboard history and notification access each show a short note with agree and not now before Android asks. Nothing is sent to us: Hyprtile has no server of its own. Only when you turn them on: the weather and the network windows you place (air quality, rain radar, news, holidays, on this day, aurora, quakes, space station, launches, rates, ping and git), the radio when you press play, the browser window and the sites you open in it, link to your own paired devices on the same wifi, and Android backup to your own Google account. Never in a backup, and not moved to a new phone: 2fa codes, the Home Assistant token, the clipboard history, health, steps, cycle, mood and habits, the link keys and pairings, the pro purchase flag (restore purchase brings pro back) and the browser's logins and cookies.
